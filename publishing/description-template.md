4. Algorithm signals (timestamps, chapters, keyword density)

## Notes on Each Section

### The First Line
YouTube shows the first 1–2 lines before "Show more." This is your second hook. Use the same line as your video hook or a close variation — it should complete the thought "I clicked because..." for the viewer. Don't waste it on "Hey welcome back."

### The Paragraph
Write this for someone who found you in search, not your subscribers. They don't know who you are. This paragraph should tell them: what the story is, why it matters, and that you have the actual documents. Name-drop the primary sources here because people search for FARA filings, IG reports, and OpenSecrets entries.

### Sources Section
Link every primary source you cited on screen. No secondary. No aggregators. If it's a court filing, link to PACER or CourtListener. If it's a 990, link to ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer. If it's a FARA filing, link directly to the FARA.gov exhibit.

This section does two things: (1) builds trust with the 15% of viewers who actually check sources, and (2) signals to YouTube that your video is about a real, documented topic.

### Chapters
Always add chapters. They improve retention (people rewatch the receipts), improve search (YouTube reads chapter titles as topic signals), and make the video feel authoritative.

Label the receipt chapters with what the receipt actually is, not just "Evidence 1." E.g.: "Receipt 1: The $3.1M NIH Grant"

### Hashtags
3–5 max. More than 5 and YouTube ignores them. Always use:
- One topic-specific tag (the story's core subject)
- One pillar tag (`#FollowTheMoney`, `#CaughtLacking`, `#DidntTellYou`, `#Receipts`, `#RapidFire`)
- One evergreen channel tag (`#LMPYOG` or `#LetMePutYouOnGame`)
